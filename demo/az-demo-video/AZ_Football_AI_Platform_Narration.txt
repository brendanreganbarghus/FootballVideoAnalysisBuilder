AZ Football AI Platform — Demo Narration
Voice: Microsoft Mark (Windows SAPI, en-US)
Audience: AZ Alkmaar
Total scenes: 8 (~4:00–4:20 target runtime)

================================================================
SCENE 1 — Opening (0:00–0:25)
================================================================
Clubs generate huge amounts of match video, but turning that footage
into reliable, structured information is still hard. This is a football
intelligence platform for AZ Alkmaar: a pipeline that takes raw match
video and turns it into validated, structured football data — and
eventually, football intelligence you can act on.

================================================================
SCENE 2 — Segmentation principle (0:25–0:55)
================================================================
We don't ask an AI model to watch an entire ninety-minute match and
guess what happened. Instead, the platform divides video into
controlled, thirty-to-sixty-second segments. Each segment is small
enough to trace, test, and validate independently — the discipline
that turns a demo trick into an engineering pipeline you can actually
trust.

================================================================
SCENE 3 — Segment builder (0:55–1:35)
================================================================
Here is the segment builder, running against real Alfheim match
footage. An operator selects a starting point and duration, and the
platform prepares a playable window — this one, fifteen to sixteen
minutes into the match. Behind the scenes it extracts frames, caches
detections, and prepares everything the analysis pipeline needs.
Segments can be grouped, replayed, and reprocessed independently, so
every result traces back to an exact, reproducible slice of video.

================================================================
SCENE 4 — AI event detection (1:35–2:15)
================================================================
Inside each segment, computer vision detects players, the ball, and
team shape, and proposes candidate football events — passes,
turnovers, shots, fouls — each with a timestamp, a team, and a
confidence score. But a raw AI prediction is a proposal, not a fact.
Every candidate still has to earn its place in the statistics by
passing through football rules validation. That principle sits
underneath everything else you'll see: predictions get questioned,
never assumed.

================================================================
SCENE 5 — Global Football Rules Reviewer & passed gate (2:15–2:55)
================================================================
This is the Global Football Rules Reviewer. Every candidate event is
checked against the actual IFAB Laws of the Game and the project's own
analytics definitions, then independently reconstructed from the video
before it's compared to the engine's output. In this fifteen-to-
sixteen-minute segment, fifteen proposals were reviewed: fourteen ended
up matching confirmed engine events exactly — most lined up directly,
and one engine event was independently verified after its proposal
didn't correspond to it. The one remaining proposal that didn't match
a distinct engine event was excluded from the published statistics,
and match-state annotations stayed out of the numbers too. All one
hundred and five protected regression tests passed before this segment
was published and locked.

================================================================
SCENE 6 — Statistics & maturity dashboard (2:55–3:25)
================================================================
Only validated events feed the statistics layer. Possession, passes,
pass completion, fouls, and interceptions are implemented and
undergoing thorough validation. Shots and shots on target are
currently in review. Corners are near completion. Offsides, attacking
and defensive actions, and player, team, and formation statistics are
still in development. We would rather show you exactly where each
statistic stands than call everything finished.

================================================================
SCENE 7 — Copilot over validated data (3:25–3:50)
================================================================
Once data is validated, Copilot can reason over it directly — how many
passes were completed, how sure are we, what still needs review.
Because it is answering from validated structured events rather than
raw predictions, the insight is only as reliable as the validation
behind it. That is exactly the point.

================================================================
SCENE 8 — Future vision & Xebia (3:50–4:15)
================================================================
The same architecture can extend to tactical patterns, scouting, and
player development — including post-processed views like player heat
maps, pitch-zone identification, and shot placement and attribution by
player, built from the full recorded clip rather than the live
statistics screen. It can also run in a private, on-premises
environment where that's required, including a future concept where a
main camera queries neighbouring goal-end and touchline cameras for a
second opinion when its own confidence is low, all on local
infrastructure rather than the cloud. This prototype was built by a
focused team to prove the concept quickly. If AZ wants to take it
further, Xebia Netherlands can bring additional AI and engineering
capacity to scale the next phase.
