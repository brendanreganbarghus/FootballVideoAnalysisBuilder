================================================================
SCENE 1 — Opening
================================================================
Clubs generate huge amounts of match video. But turning that footage into
reliable, structured information is still hard. This is a football
intelligence platform: a pipeline that turns raw match video into validated
football data, and eventually, intelligence you can act on. Here's the
current review workflow, running on real match footage.

================================================================
SCENE 2 — Segmentation and ongoing evaluation
================================================================
We don't ask an AI model to watch a ninety minute match and guess what
happened. Instead, the platform divides video into controlled, thirty to
sixty second segments, small enough to trace, test, and validate
independently. Many segments get reviewed over time. Not to train the engine
on manual clicks, but to expose where its football logic is weak. Manual
labels stay strictly evaluation only: never inference inputs, never a
timestamp exception.

================================================================
SCENE 3 — Segment builder
================================================================
Here is the segment builder inside the Football Event Review workflow,
running against real Alfheim match footage. An operator prepares a segment,
this one, fifteen to sixteen minutes in. The platform extracts frames,
caches detections, and lays out video, tracking, evidence, and review, side
by side. Segment length is configurable, typically thirty or sixty seconds,
so every result traces back to an exact slice of video.

================================================================
SCENE 4 — AI event detection
================================================================
Inside each segment, computer vision detects players, the ball, and team
shape, and proposes candidate events. Passes, turnovers, shots, fouls, each
with a timestamp, a team, and a confidence score. A raw AI prediction is a
proposal, not a fact. Every candidate is independently reconstructed from
the video before it's compared to the engine's output, and only then does it
earn its place in the statistics. Predictions get questioned, never assumed.

================================================================
SCENE 5 — Rules Reviewer and passed gate
================================================================
This is the Global Football Rules Reviewer. Every candidate event is
checked against the actual I-FAB Laws of the Game, and the project's own
analytics definitions. Then independently reconstructed from the video,
before it's compared to the engine's output. In this fifteen to sixteen
minute segment, fifteen proposals were reviewed. Fourteen matched confirmed
engine events exactly. Most lined up directly, and one engine event was
independently verified after its proposal didn't correspond to it. The one
remaining proposal that didn't match a distinct engine event was excluded
from the published statistics. And match state annotations stayed out of the
numbers too. All one hundred and five protected regression tests passed,
before this segment was published and locked.

================================================================
SCENE 6 — Statistics and maturity
================================================================
Only validated events feed the statistics layer. Possession, passes, pass
completion, fouls, and interceptions are implemented and undergoing
thorough validation. Shots and shots on target are currently in review.
Corners are near completion. Offsides, attacking and defensive actions, and
player, team, and formation statistics are still in development. We'd
rather show exactly where each statistic stands, than call everything
finished.

================================================================
SCENE 7 — Copilot over validated data
================================================================
Once data is validated, Copilot can reason over it directly. How many
passes completed, how sure we are, what still needs review. Because it
answers from validated structured events rather than raw predictions, the
insight is only as reliable as the validation behind it. That's exactly the
point.

================================================================
SCENE 8 — Future vision and continuous processing
================================================================
The same architecture can extend to tactical patterns, scouting, and player
development. Including heat maps and pitch zone views, built from the full
recorded clip. Further ahead, the same segmented, state machine driven
review is designed to eventually run continuously. Configurable thirty or
sixty second chunks, feeding the same law grounded match state engine,
appending validated statistics to the screen as more of a match is
processed. The rules engine itself could then refresh on a regular, even
daily, cadence. Always through the same evidence based, regression
protected process.

================================================================
SCENE 9 — Query By Probability
================================================================
One further concept worth naming on its own: Query By Probability. One
strong primary camera handles the main stream continuously. Only when its
own detection confidence drops, for particular frames or a short window,
does the system query the specific secondary camera that actually covers
that moment. A goal end or touchline camera, fusing its evidence back in to
strengthen the decision, then reverting to the main view. It never watches
every secondary stream for the whole match. This is architecture we're
exploring for larger, multi camera venues. A future concept, not current
behaviour.

================================================================
SCENE 10 — Partnership
================================================================
This prototype was built by a focused team, to prove the concept quickly
and honestly. Showing each statistic at its real stage of validation,
rather than dressed up as finished. If you want to take it further, Xebia
Netherlands, shown here, can bring additional AI and engineering capacity,
to help scale the next phase.
