2 · Why ours is different

0:30 – 1:00
You

"Plenty of tools draw boxes around players. Ours asks a harder question: what does the Law say just happened?"

"Our engine runs on the IFAB Laws of the Game. A pass only counts when the ball leaves one player and a teammate takes control. And when it can't prove something, it doesn't guess. It shows a dash instead of a zero, because a wrong number is worse than no number."

"Don't take my word for it. Watch it work on real match footage."

DoPoint along the four pipeline steps on the board: Capture → Context → Understand → Improve. On "watch it work", turn toward your colleague.

Safe, quotable facts

  • Demo segment: Alfheim, 04:00–05:00, 60 s. 24 engine events (19 completed passes, 5 turnovers), matched one-to-one against the reviewed set.
  • The rules engine follows a reviewed IFAB Laws of the Game profile. Passes, turnovers and shots on target are project statistics built on top of it.
  • Goal geometry comes from Law 1: 7.32 m × 2.44 m.
  • When there's no evidence, shots on target shows —, never 0.
  • Approved minutes are locked to the exact engine version and output they were checked against.

Don't overclaim

  • The Innovation demo uses frozen ball coordinates. Don't call it raw-video ball tracking or a tracking benchmark.
  • Shots-on-target thresholds are provisional, with one positive example so far. Say "early" if asked about accuracy.
  • The two-camera "asks for help" idea is future architecture. Say "future" out loud.
  • No invented audience numbers. "Thousands of matches" is a deliberately soft claim.

If a judge asks…

  • "How do you know it's right?" A human reviews every minute before it counts, and each approved minute becomes a regression test.
  • "Why not just use AI?" AI sees. The Laws decide. We need both, and we keep them separate.
  • "What do you need?" Time and support to prove it across more matches.
