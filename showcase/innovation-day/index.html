01 / Opportunity

Useful analysis should not arrive after the moment has passed.

Grassroots coaches have limited analyst time and constrained budgets. Existing sports-camera products prove the demand, but this experiment asks a different question: can camera-neutral hardware and local edge processing provide timely team-level insight while keeping control of video and recurring costs?

01

Timely coaching feedback

Publish provisional statistics near halftime and full time, when coaches can still use them.

02

Grassroots economics

Test commodity panoramic IP cameras and a shared local GPU instead of designing around a mandatory per-camera cloud ecosystem.

03

Club-controlled data

Keep raw match footage on the club network by default and publish only compact statistics to local displays.

02 / Product hypothesis

Four views. One local match state. One simple scoreboard.

The target installation combines four synchronized, fixed panoramic feeds in pitch coordinates rather than stitching a broadcast image. A local GPU proposes events; an operator remains the authority for uncertain moments.

→

→
Edge AI GPU detection, tracking and camera fusion
→

→
Outcome Review UI and local scoreboard

Target latency is 10-20 seconds for provisional figures. RTSP ingest, four-camera synchronization and production GPU throughput are planned, not yet implemented.

03 / Honest progress

Separate what works, what has evidence, and what comes next.

The current system is a recorded, single-camera research POC with live-style replay. Its validation workflow is designed to prevent a fix for one passage of play from breaking another.

Working now

Match Lab playground

Recorded-video selection, player/team/ball overlays, pitch and goal calibration, manual event controls, AI counters, event timelines and local segment processing.

Benchmark evidence

v41 pass and turnover logic

One confirmed gold reel contains 16 reviewed events. Its published team totals match that reference exactly, and executable regression tests protect the current behavior.

Evidence on a difficult research reel, not a production accuracy guarantee.
Validation in progress

Five fixed blind tests

Five one-minute reels are prepared. Manual references must be locked before AI scoring. Release requires at least 90% overall team-aware F1 and no reel below 80%.

Planned

Live multi-camera product

Four feeds, stream recovery, frame synchronization, camera fusion, reliable shots and corners, GPU latency measurement, operator queue and stadium-display publishing.

04 / Engineering journey

The hard part is not drawing a box. It is preserving football meaning.

The detector sees pixels. The product needs a durable explanation of control, flight, reception, interruption and restart across noisy observations.

A tiny, fast ball Wide panoramic footage reduces the ball to very few useful pixels.
Perspective distortion Image distance changes dramatically between near and far touchlines.
Occlusion and identity Players overlap and tracker IDs fragment or alternate during contact.
Airborne fly-bys A ball crossing a player box is not automatically a touch.
Long clearances Ownership must survive several seconds without inventing control.
Restarts and boundaries First touch, out-of-play state and last ownership must agree.
Missed visual evidence Ball deceleration and a second flight can reveal an occluded pass.
Regression risk Saved reels and exact event gates reject improvements that break prior wins.

06 / Market position

The market is validated. The architecture is the experiment.

Veo and Zone14 demonstrate clear demand for automated football recording and analysis. This concept does not claim feature parity; it investigates a local-first, camera-neutral route for clubs that value timely statistics and direct control of their footage.

Our hypothesis

Club-operated edge analytics

  • Four independently selected panoramic IP cameras
  • Local recording and GPU inference by default
  • Compact local scoreboard output
  • Operator correction for disputed events
  • Hardware reuse where video quality passes the benchmark
Competitive reality

Do not build the pitch on stale assumptions

  • Vendor bundles, regions and promotions change regularly
  • Zone14 advertises live analysis and streaming capabilities
  • Veo offers camera, subscription, analytics and live options
  • Commercial comparison requires current club-specific quotes
  • The POC must win on measured value, not unsupported claims

07 / Cost hypothesis

Benchmark the camera before buying the system.

The target is a reusable local installation rather than a finished vendor quote. Camera suitability depends on ball pixels, blur, exposure, bitrate, stream access and synchronization - not brand or headline resolution alone.

08 / Licensing and trust

Open source is a capability, not automatic commercial permission.

Code, model weights, training data and match footage each have separate rights. A paid pilot cannot proceed until all four are documented.

09 / Roadmap

Move from evidence to a consented live club pilot.

Each phase has an explicit benchmark gate. New event types do not merge if they regress the confirmed pass and turnover baseline.

Freeze passes and turnovers

Complete five blind reels and meet the 90% overall / 80% per-reel gates.

Add goal and boundary state

Validate shots, shots on target, touchline exits, goal-line exits and corners.

Prove four-camera edge ingest

Synchronize RTSP feeds, fuse pitch observations and meet latency on target hardware.

Run a governed club pilot

Use consented footage, operator review and measured coaching value across 3-5 matches.
